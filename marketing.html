SEO Management

Search performance is not a content exercise. It is a retrieval-systems problem constrained by crawl economics, index governance, link topology and render-path physics. Below is the discipline we actually run against a client estate — the surfaces we hold, the models we compute, and the cadence we hold them to.

01

Crawl Budget Allocation & Log-File Forensics

Raw server logs are ingested and segmented by user agent, then verified by reverse-DNS resolution to eliminate spoofed crawler traffic before a single conclusion is drawn. Verified hit distribution is reconciled against the canonical URL inventory to isolate crawl waste — parameterised duplicates, faceted permutations, soft-404 responses and multi-hop redirect chains consuming allocation that should be servicing revenue-bearing templates.

  • Verified-bot segmentation and spoof rejection
  • Crawl-waste isolation by template class
  • Directive arbitration across robots.txt, meta robots and X-Robots-Tag
  • Redirect-chain flattening to single-hop resolution
02

Index Coverage Governance & Canonical Integrity

Every indexable URL is reconciled across four independent sources of truth: XML sitemap declaration, internal link-graph reachability, canonical annotation, and the coverage state reported by Search Console. Divergence between any two is treated as a defect and triaged, not tolerated. The objective is a tight, defensible index footprint in which every admitted document earns its retrieval cost.

  • Canonical cluster and hreflang reciprocity validation
  • Index-bloat suppression and thin-content triage
  • Pagination, faceting and parameter-space containment
  • Soft-404 and status-code integrity enforcement
03

Information Architecture & Internal Link Equity Modelling

The estate is modelled as a directed graph and a damped random-surfer distribution is computed across it to establish where internal authority actually accumulates, as against where the commercial model requires it to sit. Architecture is then arbitrated on that evidence rather than on intuition, with click depth and anchor distribution treated as tunable parameters.

  • Depth-to-conversion path compression
  • Anchor-text distribution normalisation
  • Orphan and near-orphan reclamation
  • Hub-and-spoke consolidation of fragmented clusters
04

Semantic Entity Consolidation & Topical Authority

Query space is clustered by observed SERP overlap rather than lexical similarity, which is the only reliable way to determine what the retrieval system considers a single intent. Entity coverage is then mapped against knowledge-graph relationships to expose the gaps where a corpus fails to demonstrate topical completeness, and cannibalising documents are consolidated or differentiated on measured evidence.

  • SERP-overlap intent clustering
  • Cannibalisation detection and resolution
  • Entity gap analysis against the knowledge graph
  • Consolidation-versus-expansion arbitration
05

Render-Path Engineering & Core Web Vitals

Field data at the 75th percentile is held strictly separate from lab data; only the former is treated as ground truth. Largest Contentful Paint is decomposed into its four constituent sub-parts so remediation is aimed at the segment actually carrying the cost, Interaction to Next Paint is profiled at the long-task level, and layout instability is isolated to the specific session window responsible.

  • LCP sub-part decomposition and budget assignment
  • INP long-task profiling and main-thread yielding
  • CLS session-window isolation and reservation
  • Critical-path, preload and priority-hint governance
06

Structured Data & SERP Feature Acquisition

Schema.org markup is emitted as JSON-LD and validated against both the underlying vocabulary and the narrower eligibility constraints that govern rich-result rendering. Entities are expressed as a linked graph with stable @id references rather than isolated flat blocks, so that the machine-readable layer of the estate resolves as a single coherent structure.

  • Entity-graph construction with persistent @id resolution
  • Rich-result eligibility validation against live constraints
  • Feature displacement and real-estate modelling
  • Markup regression testing in the deployment pipeline
07

Off-Site Authority & Referring-Domain Risk

Referring-domain acquisition is assessed on velocity and topical proximity, never on raw count. Anchor-text distribution is monitored against natural-language baselines to keep the external profile inside defensible tolerances, and inbound neighbourhoods are audited for the correlated low-quality patterns that precede a manual or algorithmic devaluation.

  • Acquisition velocity and topical proximity scoring
  • Anchor distribution tolerance monitoring
  • Neighbourhood risk and correlated-pattern detection
  • Lost-link and broken-endpoint reclamation
08

Algorithmic Volatility Monitoring & Regression Forensics

Ranking, impression and click series are held against a rolling statistical baseline. Movement is scored in standard deviations from that baseline so that ordinary volatility is separated from genuine regression before anybody touches the site. When a threshold is breached, impact is attributed at segment level and isolated against the deployment log and the core-update timeline.

  • Rolling z-score anomaly promotion
  • Segment-level impact attribution
  • Core-update isolation against the deployment record
  • Defined remediation and rollback windows

The Models We Compute

These are the working formulations we run against a client estate. Several are the published algorithms themselves; the remainder are internal models we use to prioritise and to promote movement from noise to incident. All of them are computed, versioned and reported — none of them are decorative.

Internal Equity

Damped Internal Authority Distribution

PR(u) = (1 − d) / N + d · Σv ∈ B(u) PR(v) / L(v)

Governs — where internal authority accumulates across the estate, iterated to convergence over the directed internal link graph.

d = 0.85 damping factor · N = indexable document count · B(u) = the set of documents linking to u · L(v) = outbound degree of v

Crawl Economics

Crawl Budget Efficiency

CBE = Rcanonical / Rtotal

Governs — the proportion of verified crawler requests landing on canonical, indexable, revenue-bearing URLs. The residual is allocation being consumed by duplicates, parameter space and redirect chains.

Computed per template class over a rolling 30-day log window, segmented by verified user agent

Lexical Retrieval

Okapi BM25 Relevance Weighting

score(D,Q) = Σi=1n IDF(qi) · f(qi,D) · (k1 + 1)f(qi,D) + k1 · (1 − b + b · |D| / avgdl)
IDF(qi) = ln( (N − n(qi) + 0.5) / (n(qi) + 0.5) + 1 )

Governs — term saturation and document-length normalisation across the corpus, used to identify under- and over-weighted documents relative to the ranking set.

k1 ∈ [1.2, 2.0] saturation · b = 0.75 length normalisation · avgdl = mean document length. This is a probabilistic relevance model, not keyword density.

Visual Stability

Cumulative Layout Shift

CLS = maxw ∈ W Σs ∈ w ( impact fractions × distance fractions )

Governs — visual stability at the 75th percentile of field traffic, scored on the worst qualifying burst rather than the lifetime total.

W = session windows capped at 5s duration with a 1s gap tolerance · shifts are excluded within 500ms of a qualifying input

Render Path

LCP Sub-Part Decomposition

LCP = TTFB + RLD + RLT + ERD

Governs — which quarter of the render path is actually carrying the cost, so that remediation budget is assigned against evidence rather than against the total.

TTFB = time to first byte · RLD = resource load delay · RLT = resource load time · ERD = element render delay

Demand Capture

Weighted Visibility Index

V = Σk vk · CTR(pk)Σk vk

Governs — the share of addressable demand actually being captured, weighted by position-dependent click-through rather than by flat ranking counts.

vk = demand volume for query k · pk = observed position · CTR(p) = position curve calibrated against the estate's own Search Console data, not industry averages

Sequencing

Opportunity Prioritisation Score

O = v · [ CTR(pt) − CTR(pc) ] · r · mD

Governs — the order of works. Effort is sequenced by marginal contribution margin per unit of difficulty, not by search volume.

v = demand volume · pt = target position · pc = current position · r = conversion rate · m = contribution margin · D = normalised competitive difficulty

Regression Detection

Volatility Promotion Threshold

z = ( xt − μ[t−n, t−1] ) / σ[t−n, t−1]

Governs — the point at which a movement stops being volatility and becomes an incident. |z| ≥ 2.5 against a 28-day rolling baseline promotes to investigation and attribution.

Computed per segment and per template class so that estate-level averages cannot mask a localised regression
