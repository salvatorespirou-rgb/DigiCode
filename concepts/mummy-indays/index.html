Our Table

Cooked the way it is
cooked at home.

We are a family from Negros Oriental, in the Visayan Islands, and we cook the food we grew up eating — not a lighter version of it, and not a version explained for anyone. The caldereta is the caldereta. The dinugo-an is made Tagalog or Cebuano, whichever way you ask for it.

Everything is cooked to order for your date, in the quantities your guest list actually needs, and it arrives hot in trays sized so nobody is scraping the corners by the time the last table gets up.

Filipino food is not really served. It is set down in the middle and everybody reaches. The way we were taught to feed people

Meet the Family

A tight-knit family,
feeding yours.

We come from Negros Oriental, in the Visayan Islands, and everything we cook comes with us from there. The recipes are the family’s own. The way we lay a table is the way we were taught to lay one.

Every dish is prepared by hand and every event is run by people whose name is on it — which is the whole point. Sit down to our cooking and you are getting our heritage, our traditions and our idea of hospitality, served the way we would serve it at home.

Bringing that to our friends in Sydney is the reason we started, and it is still the reason we do it.

— The family at Mummy Inday’s
